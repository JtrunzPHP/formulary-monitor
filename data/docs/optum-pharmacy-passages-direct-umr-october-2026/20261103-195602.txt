FDA approves Hovilpri for use in herpes simplex virus infection
On Oct. 9, 2026, the FDA approved Hovilpri (pritelivir) for the treatment of mucocutaneous lesions in
immunocompromised adults caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection that is refractory (with or without
documented resistance) to treatment with acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir.
HSV is a common, lifelong viral infection caused by HSV-1 and HSV-2 virus. Many people with HSV have no
symptoms, while symptomatic infections cause clusters of mucocutaneous lesions or fluid-filled blisters that
rupture into sores. HSV is not curable and can be in a dormant or active state – antiviral therapy is used to treat
individual outbreaks or suppress the infection to reduce the number of symptomatic recurrences and lower the
risk of transmission to others. Individuals who are immunocompromised can have more frequent and serious HSV
outbreaks that may not respond well to available oral antiviral treatments due to resistance.
Hovilpri provides a novel oral option for these individuals to control the infection. By inhibiting a different viral
protein, it is an effective option for those who have developed resistance to antiviral drugs such as acyclovir,
valacyclovir, and famciclovir.
The Optum Rx National Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee is thoroughly assessing Hovilpri for clinical value
and safety. Afterwards, Optum Rx will determine its place on Optum Rx standard formularies.
